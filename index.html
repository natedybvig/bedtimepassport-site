How a night goes

Every story follows the same gentle rhythm, so bedtime turns into a ritual your child can count on. The adventure comes first. Then the sentences get softer and longer, and everyone in the story goes to sleep too.

Each night opens with a short Parent Quick Guide: the capital, how to say the tricky words, and five quick facts written so you can read them to your child too.

  1. Goggles on!

    Mossy’s magic words. Your child makes circles with their fingers, holds them over their eyes like flying goggles, and off you go.

  2. Something to wonder about

    A ship that sailed for twenty minutes. A beetle that does a headstand to drink the fog. A train with a bird’s beak. And always a laugh, usually at Mossy’s expense.

  3. The slow-down

    The last few paragraphs get quieter, and every animal you met settles in for the night.

  4. Whisper it with me

    Every story ends with good night in a local language. Góða nótt in Iceland. Usiku mwema in Tanzania. Whisper it together.

  5. A Dream Seed

    A short, closed-eyes picture to read slowly as the lights go out. Nothing to answer, nothing to discuss. Just sleep.

Read a little

This is how Night 1 begins, the night your child meets Mossy for the first time.

“Evening, explorer!” says the moose, in a deep, rumbly voice. “I’m Mossy. Captain Mossy, if you like. This is your Bedtime Passport. It only works when you’re sleepy. And every night, it will fly us somewhere amazing.”

“Fly?” you whisper. “But moose can’t fly.”

Mossy peeks at you over his goggles. “Moose,” he says, “can do all sorts of things when nobody is watching. When I say the magic words, pull your goggles down. Like this.” He holds two hoof-circles over his eyes. “Ready? Goggles on!”

WHOOOOSH! Your bedroom folds up like a paper map, and you are flying.

About the author

Daly Henrikson is a children’s author, raconteur and part-time mammal from Saint Paul, Minnesota. His two sons are the inspiration for Mossy, and they have heard nearly every story in the series at bedtime.

He believes every country on Earth deserves a bedtime story, and that every story is better read aloud by someone who loves you.
